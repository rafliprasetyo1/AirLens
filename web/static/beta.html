    How it works

    1. 4 MQ sensorsHeated sensors whose resistance (Rs) falls when gas reaches them: MQ-2 (smoke), MQ-3 (alcohol), MQ-7 (CO indication) and MQ-135 (air quality/VOC).
    2. ESP32Reads the four sensors through voltage dividers every 2 seconds.
    3. InternetSends each reading to AirLens over HTTPS.
    4. AirLensLearns each sensor's clean-air baseline, turns readings into ratios, runs the model and explains it with SHAP.

    After switching on, the sensors warm up for 3 minutes. AirLens then takes the median of the next 24 readings as each sensor's clean-air baseline. An alert starts when the model is at least 70% sure of smoke twice in a row, and ends once it is at least 60% sure the air is clean again, so it doesn't flicker. Alcohol-like vapours are shown but do not raise an alert, because the training data could barely tell them from clean air.

    How the model decides

    The model is a small decision-tree ensemble (LightGBM) that looks at five numbers: each sensor's ratio to clean air, plus MQ-3 divided by MQ-2. SHAP splits every decision into the push from each of those numbers, so the parts always add up to the model's score.

    Training data: MultimodalGasData (Narkhede et al., Data 2022, 7(8):112; Mendeley Data, CC BY 4.0).

    Limitations

    • The model was trained on readings from another team's sensors, recorded in one session per gas. Our own sensors can behave differently, which is why we record calibration data at the lab.
    • MQ sensors are not selective: each one reacts to many gases. “Alcohol” here means alcohol-like vapours such as sanitizer or perfume, and in the training data even the alcohol sensor (MQ-3) barely told perfume from clean air.
    • MQ-7 normally needs a heating cycle to measure CO. Here it runs at a constant heat, so its reading is only an indication.
    • This is not a CO alarm or a certified smoke detector. It does not measure PM2.5 or any concentration in ppm.
    • The baseline assumes the air was clean while it was being learned.

    Readings from the simulator are always labelled “Simulated”.
